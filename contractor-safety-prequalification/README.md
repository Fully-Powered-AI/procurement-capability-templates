# Contractor safety prequalification

For a named job, the operations, facilities or site manager gets a safety questionnaire scaled to the risk of the work, then a record of the contractor's answers scored only on the evidence supplied, with every gap, the conditions that close it and a review date. A competent person decides.

*Workflow template · Procurement and suppliers · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/contractor-safety-prequalification/)*

## The outcome

Letting a contractor onto your site stops depending on a friendly phone call and a confident answer. The questions fit the job. A firm cleaning the offices answers four; a crew working from a platform above a busy warehouse floor answers every one, because the single riskiest task decides how hard the whole job is looked at.

When the answers come back, each question is scored on what is attached and nothing else. A promise with no certificate behind it is a gap. An answer that leans on harnesses and experience is a gap. A card that runs out two days before the job ends is caught weeks ahead, not on the morning work starts. Insurance is set against your own minimums, with the shortfall in dollars.

The record then shows what your rules point to, the condition that would close each gap and who chases it, the checks only a person can make, such as confirming a certificate with the insurer, and the date the approval should be looked at again. Your competent person reads it and decides: approve, approve with conditions, or reject. The assistant sends nothing, approves nothing and adds no one to a supplier list.

## Who starts the work, and when

The operations, facilities or site manager bringing in a contractor for a named job starts it as soon as the job, the site and the dates are known. The first run produces the questionnaire, which a person sends. The second run, once the signed answers and documents are back, produces the evaluation record.

It runs again when an approval reaches its review date, or when the job changes and picks up a riskier task. The decision belongs to the person your company names as competent to judge contractor safety, such as its health and safety manager or an outside adviser.

## What they supply

- **A short job brief:** the contractor, the work and each activity in it, the site, the dates, who else could be affected nearby, who decides and who chases conditions.
- **Your settings:** the activities you treat as high-risk, your insurance minimums, the questions that must pass, the findings that stop an approval and how long an approval lasts. Sensible defaults are supplied for each.
- **The contractor's signed answers and documents,** each marked with the question it answers.
- **A list of the people on the job** with their certificates and expiry dates, in five stated columns.

No connection to email, a supplier system, an insurer or any register is needed, and none is asked for.

## What comes out

The first run gives a questionnaire ready to send: the job, its risk tier and why, plain instructions for the contractor, and each question with the evidence that answers it.

The second run gives one evaluation record, in a fixed order:

1. **The risk tier,** activity by activity, and the one that set it.
2. **The evidence register:** every question scored Met, Partly met or Gap, with the document behind it and the reason for any shortfall.
3. **Insurance against your minimums,** with any shortfall and every expiry date checked against the job's last day.
4. **Claims not counted,** quoted in the contractor's words.
5. **Stop findings,** then **what your rules point to,** with work on any high-risk activity held until its questions are met.
6. **Conditions,** each with the evidence that closes it, a date and who chases it.
7. **Checks for a person,** **points for your competent person** and **the review date.**
8. **A blank decision line** for the competent person to sign.

People named in the contractor's incident records appear as Worker 1, Worker 2, with no injury or health detail.

## What has to change for your company

- **Who decides.** Name your competent person for contractor safety, and who stands in when they are away.
- **Your high-risk activities.** Start from the default list and add the work your sites see.
- **Your insurance minimums,** by kind of job if they differ.
- **Your rules.** Which questions must pass, which findings stop an approval, or your own scoring scheme in their place.
- **Your timing.** Whether every condition closes before work starts, and how long an approval lasts.
- **Your adviser.** The workflow template gives no view on what the law asks of your company, on liability or on contract wording. It names those questions for whoever advises you.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Decide which documents the assistant may read.
