# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. It draws on the contractor prequalification package in ashley-eyekyam/hse-leadership-skills (Apache-2.0). From that package it takes a questionnaire to send and a scored record of the answers, and three tiers of questions, with one dangerous task enough to put a whole job in the top tier. It takes a core set of questions for every contractor, more for skilled trades and an added set for each dangerous activity, each with the document that answers it. It takes scoring only what the documents show, so an unsupported answer is a gap, with the claim, the evidence and its status kept for each item, and it refuses, as the package does, to pass dangerous work on protective equipment or experience alone. Nothing is scored until the contractor, the job and the site are named and the insurance, safety record and trade competence documents are in; the brief also asks who else could be affected and whether the approval is new or a renewal. The outcome is approve, conditional or reject: rejection when a point fails beyond repair, conditions that carry an owner and a date, work held until they close, and the decision left to a competent person. The review falls at the first expiry or after a set period, whichever comes first, and expired documents count as gaps again on renewal. Also from the package: certificates checked with the body that issued them; people in incident records reduced to labels; questions on supervision and subcontracting; a stated scoring rule with a named decision maker; a return date for the answers and a fresh look at the questions when the job changes; first aid on site as a question a company may add; a questionnaire that opens with directions to the contractor, closes with a signed declaration and asks about rescue plans, permits, inspection of access equipment and controls ahead of protective equipment; and the order of the record. Its hard cases (a certificate claimed but not supplied, one contractor on a low-risk and a high-risk job, an injured worker named in the records) are used here as acceptance tests in new scenarios. Fully Powered AI removed the references to standards and national rules. It added Met, Partly met and Gap with what each means, including evidence that runs out before the job ends; must-pass questions and stop findings as company settings, with a mechanical outcome rule and a blank decision line; insurance set against the company's own minimums, like with like, with the shortfall; and trade competence scored apart from the certification for each dangerous activity. It also added the people list with stated column headers and a stop when one does not match; each claim not counted, quoted in the contractor's words; the checks only a person can make, listed apart; points the rules cannot settle, such as an incident that concerns an activity in the job; owners and dates taken only from the brief and settings; the review date counted from the job's last day and set again when conditions close; and declining questions of law, liability, insurance cover and contract wording. No third-party text is reused.
