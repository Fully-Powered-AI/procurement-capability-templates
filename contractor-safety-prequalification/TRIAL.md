# Contractor safety prequalification: a trial on its own example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs were the invented example in `public/example.md`: the job brief and settings for the first run; the two dates, the contractor's answers, documents E1 to E8 and the people list for the second. No client data was used.

## How the trial was run, and why it is not blind

This is the second run of the template on its example, and it replaces the record of the first. The first run, earlier the same day by another model, found that question C4 could be scored two ways (it counted a platform operator card under both C4 and H1, where the example counted it under H1 only), that the example output gave an issue date and a receipt date its inputs did not hold, that one reason in the example said more than document E7 showed, and ten places where the template left the reader to guess. An independent review then failed the template on those points.

I made the fixes myself before this run: C4 now asks only about trade qualifications, and a platform card or other certificate for a dangerous activity counts under that activity's H1; the issue and receipt dates became inputs, with "date not given" when they are missing, and the example's inputs now carry both; and the template now settles the insurance basis, names inside quoted claims and the signatory's name, what "the outcome" of an incident keeps, C3 naming a role, the decision type's wider scope, the site permit in H3, and a supervisor who also does high-hazard work. I then followed the fenced instruction block and the output format as they now stand, on the example's inputs only, with the first run as at 9 November 2026 and the second as at 18 November 2026.

The run is not blind and is not an independent test. I had read the template, the example's published output and the first run's record before running it, and I revised the example's output to match the revised rules. Where my output matches the example, that may be because I had seen it. The ambiguities listed at the end are the more useful signal.

## What was observed

Every part the output format requires was produced in both runs, with no placeholder left unfilled and no stop: the job, site, dates and activities were given, the electrical work was stated as isolated, and the people-list headers matched. Dates and amounts were checked against the documents: 7 December 2026 is a Monday, 11 December a Friday, 10 and 17 November are Tuesdays and 18 November is a Wednesday.

Against the published example output there were no differences in scores, counts, must-pass list, outcome, conditions, checks, points or review date, which is expected, since I revised that output myself (see above).

The first run's findings, checked against this run:

1. C4 scored twice: settled. C4 is Met, the card is scored under H1 only, and there are five conditions, not six.
2. Issue and receipt dates not in the inputs: settled. Both are now inputs and are printed as given.
3. The T1 reason saying more than E7 shows: settled. The reason now says E7 is general and names no site, platform or job.
4. "Evidence: none supplied" for H2 and H3 when an answer and E7 were supplied: settled. The register says what was supplied and why it did not count.
5. T2's attendance statement: settled. It is listed as a claim not counted and does not change the score.
6. The ten ambiguities the first run listed: the insurance basis, names in quoted claims, the outcome of an incident, C3 naming a role, the decision type's wider scope, the site permit and the supervisor who also operates the platform are now covered by the template. "Prepared by" still takes the role that ran the template, as before.

Where the template still left me to judge:

1. **Typed summaries without a holder's name.** E3 and E6 are typed summaries that do not say in whose name the citation or the cards were issued. The template's "for this contractor" test assumes the name is visible. I treated the person's statement that the originals are on file as enough and raised no point.
2. **The review date "that would apply" to later work.** Step 14 asks for the review date that would apply to a wider scope; I used this record's review date, because the template gives no other.
3. **T1 with two shortfalls.** E7 is general and there is no risk assessment. Both put T1 at Partly met, so the score did not depend on the choice, but the template does not say whether a missing risk assessment alone would be Partly met or a Gap.

Reviewer checklist: items 1 to 7 and 9 hold for both runs on these inputs. Item 8 (checks made or assigned before the decision) cannot be tested by a run; the record only lists them. Nothing was sent, approved or added to any list, and the decision line was blank.

This was one run by one model on one invented example, after the same model had revised the template and the example. It shows the revised instructions can be followed to a complete record and that the C4 question now has one reading on these inputs; it does not show that two reviewers or two models would reach the same scores.

## After the run

Everything above is the record of the run as made; none of it has been changed. An independent review the same day passed the template and noted that this record is a summary of the output rather than the output itself, and that it comes from the same model that revised the template and the example; a blind run in a fresh session would be stronger evidence and has not yet been made. After the review, without a further run:

- The three judgements listed above were written into the template, each the way this run made it: a method statement or a risk assessment for T1 without the other is Partly met; a typed summary the person says is on file counts even where it does not show the holder's name; a wider decision type takes this record's review date.
- The example's incident summary (E2), an invented input, gained the line "2023 (November, December): no reportable injuries", so that every month of the period it covers is accounted for. The run above used the input without that line; C2 was Met either way.
- The example's list of documents now shows E7 answering H2 as well as T1, since its evidence register cites E7 under H2. This record does not say how the run listed E7.

None of these changes a score, a condition, a check, a point or the review date on the example.
