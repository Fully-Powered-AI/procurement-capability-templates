# Vendor quote comparison

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare one comparison sheet for a purchase that has several quotes, so the person who chooses can see exactly why each vendor is still in contention or has dropped out. The criteria are written down and agreed before any quote is compared. Every quote is then costed the same way over the same period, and the vendors are taken through three rounds of elimination: must-haves, the budget ceiling, and outperformed (another vendor is at least as good on everything and costs no more). Each vendor ends with one standing and its reason. The assistant prepares and checks. It never names a winner, contacts a vendor, accepts a quote, places an order or says what a contract term means.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when two or more quotes are in for the same purchase and someone has to choose: a service contract such as cleaning, maintenance or security, equipment, a software subscription, a supplier of a regular material. Use it again when a vendor answers a question or sends a revised quote, so every vendor is judged on the same sheet.

Do not use it:

- with fewer than two quotes. One quote can be checked against the must-haves by hand;
- before the criteria exist. The person who chooses sets them first; the assistant can list the questions that set them, and never derives criteria from the quotes themselves;
- to read, explain or negotiate contract terms. Those go, quoted word for word, to [your contract reviewer];
- to check a vendor's financial standing, references or reputation beyond what the person supplies.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the office manager, buyer or owner who gathered the quotes.
- **Sets the criteria and makes the choice:** [your decision maker], usually the owner or the budget holder. The same person approves any change to the criteria.
- **Reads the contract terms before anything is signed:** [your contract reviewer], such as the company's lawyer, an outside adviser or the owner.
- **Sends questions to vendors:** a person, after reading the drafts on the sheet.

## Inputs

1. **The purchase brief.** What is being bought and what prompted it, the start date wanted, the date the choice must be made, and the length of the commitment wanted. If the brief is missing, stop and ask for it.
2. **The criteria sheet,** agreed with [your decision maker] before the quotes are compared. If it is missing, stop. Offer the five questions in step 1 of the procedure and nothing else. It holds:
   - **Must-haves:** each written as a condition a quote either meets or does not, with a short code (M1, M2). Example: "cleaning starts no earlier than 6:00 pm".
   - **Comparison criteria:** each with a short code (C1, C2), the measure and its unit, and which way is better ("lower is better", "higher is better", or "yes is better"). Cost over the term is always the first comparison criterion and needs no line of its own.
   - **The budget ceiling** for cost over the term. If there is none, write "no ceiling set" and skip round 2.
   - **The cost period:** how many months the cost is counted over. If it is missing, use the commitment length in the brief and say so; if that is missing too, stop and ask.
   - **What every price must cover:** the items each vendor's price must include or price separately, such as supplies, delivery or installation.
   - **The date the criteria were agreed** and by whom.
3. **The quotes,** one per vendor, as the vendor sent them: a document, an email or a typed copy. Each is given a letter (Vendor A, Vendor B) in the order supplied. If a quote has no date or no price, the vendor goes on hold for that reason.
4. **Notes from calls and emails** with vendors (optional). Anything in them that is not in the quote is listed as not counted. If none are supplied, write "no notes supplied" in the header.
5. **The currency.** If quotes are in different currencies, stop and ask for the rate to use.

The assistant works only from what the person supplies. It needs no access to email, a purchasing system or any vendor's portal, and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the criteria first.** Confirm there is a brief, at least one must-have or comparison criterion beyond cost, a cost period and a date the criteria were agreed. If the criteria sheet is missing, return only these five questions for [your decision maker]: what would rule a vendor out; which qualities count among the vendors that qualify; the most you will spend over the period; how long you want to commit for; what every price must include. Compare nothing until the answers are back.
2. **List the quotes.** For each vendor: its letter, the date of the quote, the date it is valid until (or "validity not stated"), and the start date it offers. A validity given as a number of days is counted from the quote date, and the line says so, for example "valid until 18 November 2026 (14 days from the quote date)". If a quote expires before the decision date in the brief, mark it "EXPIRES BEFORE THE DECISION DATE".
3. **Cost every quote over the same period.** Cost over the term = one-off charges + each recurring charge for every period inside the cost period + every item on the "every price must cover" list that the vendor prices separately. Where a quote states a price change inside the cost period, such as a rise from a given month, count each period at the price that applies then. Show the arithmetic on one line per vendor. Where a required item is neither included nor priced, write "not priced: [item]" and do not complete the total; the vendor goes on hold. Never estimate a price the quote does not give.
4. **Fill the side-by-side.** For each vendor and each must-have and comparison criterion, write what the quote says, in its own words where they are short, or "not stated". A figure in a different unit from the criterion is converted only when the conversion is exact (for example 2 days = 48 hours); otherwise write what the quote says and mark it "not in the criterion's unit". Note anything the quote includes that the brief did not ask for as "also offered, not compared". Where a quote states a commitment length different from the one in the brief, such as month to month against 12 months, end that vendor's line with: Commitment offered: "[the quote's words]"; wanted: [length]. It is not a criterion: the cost is still counted over the cost period, and the term goes to [your contract reviewer] in step 11.
5. **Sort out what does not count.** A statement that bears on a criterion and appears only in the notes, not in the quote, is "not in writing". A claim the vendor makes about itself with nothing supplied to support it (a rating, a satisfaction figure, a certification) is "vendor's own claim, not checked". Neither counts toward any criterion. List both under "Not counted".
6. **Round 1, must-haves.** A vendor whose quote shows it does not meet a must-have is eliminated, with the must-have and the quote's words. Hours, dates and amounts the quote states count as what it offers: crews that "work 5:00 pm to 9:00 pm" work from 5:00 pm. Where the quote's words do not settle whether a must-have is met, the vendor goes on hold with a question; it is not eliminated. A vendor whose quote is silent on a must-have, or meets it only in the notes, goes on hold with a question to the vendor; it is not eliminated. A vendor whose quote is for visibly different work from the brief goes on hold here as "different scope", with what differs. All others pass.
7. **Round 2, budget.** For vendors that passed round 1: a vendor whose cost over the term is above the budget ceiling is eliminated, with its cost and the amount over. A vendor whose cost could not be completed goes on hold.
8. **Round 3, outperformed.** Compare only the vendors that passed rounds 1 and 2 with every value known. A vendor is eliminated as outperformed when another of these vendors costs no more over the term, is at least as good on every comparison criterion, and is better on at least one (cost included). Name the vendor that outperforms it and the figures. A criterion marked "not stated" for either vendor means that pair cannot be compared, and neither is eliminated by the other. Vendors on hold take no part in round 3; they are compared when their answers arrive and the sheet is run again.
9. **Write where each vendor stands.** Every vendor has exactly one standing: eliminated in round 1, 2 or 3, with its reason; on hold, with what is missing; or shortlisted. For a vendor on hold, also give its cost over the term if known.
10. **Describe the shortlist.** For each shortlisted vendor, the comparison criteria on which it is better than every other shortlisted vendor, and those on which it is worse, with the figures. Use the criteria's own measures. Name no winner and rank nothing beyond what the rounds show. If one vendor remains, say so. If none remains, write "No vendor remains" and set out the choices for [your decision maker]: relax a named criterion, ask for new quotes, or do not buy now.
11. **Collect the contract terms.** For every shortlisted vendor and every vendor on hold, quote word for word each term about the length of the commitment, renewal, notice, cancellation, price changes, early-exit charges, liability or payment. Add no reading of what they mean. If a quote has none, write "no such terms in the quote". These go to [your contract reviewer] before anything is signed.
12. **Draft the questions for vendors.** One question per gap that keeps a vendor on hold, one per "not in writing" statement on a criterion, and one per quote that expires before the decision date. Each names the vendor, the criterion and what would settle it. They are drafts for a person to send.
13. **List the decisions.** For [your decision maker]: choose among the shortlist, wait for the answers on hold, or take one of the choices for an empty shortlist. Add every expiring quote with its date. End with the line that the contract reviewer reads the terms before signing.
14. **Return the sheet** to the person who ran it, for [your decision maker]. If the criteria change after the quotes were read, record the change, the date and who approved it on the sheet, and run every round again from the start.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No brief, no criteria sheet, or no cost period:** stop and ask. Offer the five questions in step 1. Never write criteria from the quotes.
- **Fewer than two quotes:** stop; there is nothing to compare.
- **Quotes in different currencies:** stop and ask for the rate.
- **Two quotes for visibly different work** (a different site, a different quantity, a different service level from the brief): put the vendor whose quote differs from the brief on hold as "different scope", with what differs, and draft a question.
- **Asked which vendor to choose, or to rank the shortlist:** decline. Set out the shortlist and its trade-offs; [your decision maker] chooses.
- **Asked to change a criterion after seeing the results:** do it only when the person confirms [your decision maker] approves; record the change on the sheet and run every round again.
- **Asked what a contract term means, whether it is fair or enforceable, or to propose new wording:** decline. Quote the term for [your contract reviewer].
- **Asked to contact a vendor, accept a quote, place an order or sign anything:** decline and say who does it.
- **A quote contains bank details or asks for payment:** do not repeat the bank details on the sheet. Note that a payment request is outside this comparison.

## Reviewer checklist

1. The criteria on the sheet are the ones agreed before the quotes were compared, with the date; any later change is recorded. (Decision maker)
2. Every cost line uses the same period, counts each price change at the month it applies, and adds correctly. (Person who ran it)
3. Every value in the side-by-side matches the quote, and "not stated" appears wherever the quote is silent. (Person who ran it)
4. No promise from a call or an email, and no unsupported claim, has been counted toward a criterion. (Decision maker)
5. Every vendor has exactly one standing, and every elimination gives its round, the criterion and the figures or words behind it. (Decision maker)
6. No vendor was eliminated for something its quote is silent on; those are on hold. (Decision maker)
7. Every outperformed vendor is matched with the vendor that outperforms it, and the figures show it is no better on any criterion. (Person who ran it)
8. The sheet names no winner, and the contract terms are quoted word for word with no reading added. (Contract reviewer)
9. The status line is present, and nothing was sent, accepted, ordered or signed. (Decision maker)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run as a test. The trial on the invented example, recorded in public/trial.md, met cases like tests 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 11; read that record for what it showed and what it did not.

1. **No criteria.** Quotes supplied with no criteria sheet. Expected: stops and returns the five questions; compares nothing.
2. **Fails a must-have.** A quote says crews start at 5:00 pm; the must-have is no earlier than 6:00 pm. Expected: eliminated in round 1 with the quote's words.
3. **Silent on a must-have.** A quote does not mention insurance; the call notes say the vendor is "fully insured". Expected: on hold, the statement listed as not in writing, a question drafted; not eliminated.
4. **Price rise inside the period.** $1,950 a month rising 4% from month 7, over 12 months. Expected: $11,700 + $12,168 = $23,868 before other items.
5. **Required item priced separately.** Supplies at $140 a month. Expected: $1,680 added over 12 months.
6. **Required item not priced.** Expected: "not priced", total not completed, vendor on hold in round 2.
7. **Over the ceiling.** Cost $31,200 against a $28,000 ceiling. Expected: eliminated in round 2, $3,200 over.
8. **Outperformed.** Vendor D costs $27,600 with 5 restroom days and a 24-hour fix; Vendor A costs $26,400 with the same two values. Expected: D eliminated in round 3, outperformed by A, with the figures.
9. **Trade-off.** One vendor is cheaper and faster; another services more days. Expected: both shortlisted, each with what it is better and worse on; no winner named.
10. **Not stated blocks the comparison.** A criterion not stated in one of two quotes. Expected: that pair listed as not comparable; neither eliminated by the other.
11. **Expiring quote.** Valid until 18 November; decision needed by 20 November. Expected: marked on the quote line, a question drafted, and the date listed under decisions.
12. **Asked to choose.** "Just tell me which one." Expected: declines; sets out the shortlist and trade-offs.
13. **Asked about a clause.** "Is this auto-renewal fair?" Expected: declines; the term is quoted for the contract reviewer.
14. **Nobody remains.** Every vendor eliminated. Expected: "No vendor remains" with the three choices.

## What to customize

- [your decision maker] and [your contract reviewer]: the roles that choose and that read contract terms.
- Your standard must-haves for repeat purchases, such as insurance cover or a start date, kept as a starting list the decision maker edits each time.
- Your usual cost period by kind of purchase: twelve months for a service contract, the expected life for equipment.
- The list of items every price must cover for each kind of purchase.
- Whether a budget ceiling is always set, and who may raise it.
- Which contract terms your reviewer wants quoted, if the list in step 11 is too long or too short.
- Where the approved sheet is kept, so the next purchase of the same kind starts from it.
- Which documents the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
