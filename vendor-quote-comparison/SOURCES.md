Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. From the vendor-evaluation method in GTMify/aigtm (MIT) it takes opening with the purchase itself, the reason for it, the money available and how long the business will be tied in; comparing total cost over the life of the commitment rather than the first price; backing every judgment with evidence from the quote; calling out quotes that are not like for like and questions a vendor left unanswered; treating promises not written into the quote as worth nothing; drawing attention to renewal and exit terms; and accepting that sometimes the business should not buy at all yet. From the elimination-research method in glebis/claude-skills (MIT) it takes settling the criteria and the lines that rule an option out before any option is assessed; removing options over a budget ceiling from the decision while keeping them visible; counting recurring costs over a fixed period; setting aside an option that another beats or matches on every measure at no higher cost; and keeping gaps and caveats on the record instead of dropping them. Fully Powered AI designed the three rounds of elimination with one standing and one reason for every vendor, the rule that a quote silent on a must-have puts a vendor on hold rather than out, the record of any change to the criteria after the quotes are read, the contract terms quoted word for word for a reviewer without interpretation, and the rule that the assistant names no winner. No third-party text is reused.
