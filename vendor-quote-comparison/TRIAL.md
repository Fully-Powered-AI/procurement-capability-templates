## What was observed

- **Every required part was produced,** in the format's order, including the status line. Nothing in the inputs triggered a stop condition: the brief, criteria sheet, cost period and currency were all present, and there were six quotes.
- **Differences from the published example:** none in the standings, figures or shortlist. The wording of two vendor questions and the "where each vendor stands" lines differs slightly. The example's second decision names a deadline ("decide by 18 November"); this run said the same thing in slightly different words.
- **Facts not in the inputs:** none found in either output. One is derived, not stated: Vendor A's "valid for 14 days" from a quote dated 4 November became "valid until 18 November". Both outputs do this.
- **Shortcoming in the trial itself.** The method asked for the example's output to stay unread until after the run. A command that was meant to stop at the end of the inputs printed the first half of the example's sheet (to the "Not counted" section) before the run began. The costs, side-by-side and not-counted parts therefore are not an independent check. The round results, shortlist, contract terms, questions and decisions were worked out after that point but without seeing the example's versions, and they agree with it. Read the agreement on the first half as weaker evidence.
- **Where the instructions were ambiguous:**
  - Round 1 on Vendor B: "Our crews work 5:00 pm to 9:00 pm" does not say cleaning starts at 5:00. It was treated as a failure of M1 because the quote's words show work from 5:00 pm. The template does not say whether that reading is a fail or a question.
  - "Valid for 14 days" has no stated starting point; the run counted from the quote date.
  - The format's "Vendor [letter]" list line is placed before the criteria, but step 2 calls for the lines after the header; the run followed the format.
  - Vendor E's quote is month to month, while the brief wants a 12-month commitment. The template costs over the period set and says nothing about a vendor that offers different terms from the commitment wanted.
  - Step 11 says to quote terms for shortlisted and on-hold vendors, but the format example is not clear on whether a term such as "month to month" counts as one about commitment length. It was quoted.
  - The step 12 rule gives one question per "not in writing" statement on a criterion. Vendor C's rating claim bears on no criterion, so it got no question.
- **Nothing was told to stop for.** No contract term was interpreted, no winner named, and no vendor contacted.

## Changed after this run, and not run again

Changed on 9 November 2026 after an independent review of the published template, and not run again. The output and the observations above are as recorded and describe the template and the example as they stood at the run. No change alters a figure, a standing, the shortlist or a count in either output.

- **Must-haves read from the quote's words.** The procedure and the pasted instructions now say that hours, dates and amounts a quote states count as what it offers, so crews that "work 5:00 pm to 9:00 pm" work from 5:00 pm, which is how this run and the example read Vendor B. They also say that where a quote's words do not settle whether a must-have is met, the vendor goes on hold with a question. The example's line for Vendor B under "Where each vendor stands" now reads "crews work from 5:00 pm", as this run did, in place of "crews start at 5:00 pm", which said more than the quote.
- **Validity given in days.** A validity given as a number of days is now counted from the quote date, as both outputs did, and the quote line says so. The example's line for Vendor A now reads "valid until 18 November 2026 (14 days from the quote date)".
- **A different commitment from the one wanted.** Where a quote states a commitment length different from the brief's, the side-by-side line now ends with the commitment offered in the quote's words and the length wanted; it is not a criterion, and the cost is still counted over the cost period. The example's side-by-side line for Vendor E now ends: Commitment offered: "Service is month to month"; wanted: 12 months.
- **The pasted instructions** were rewrapped where one line ran long, and the acceptance tests now point to this record.

The other places listed above where the template was unclear (the order of the quote lines in the format, whether "month to month" counts as a term on commitment length for the contract reviewer, and no question for a claim that bears on no criterion) are unchanged. The first half of the example's sheet having been seen before the run, as recorded above, also still stands: a clean re-run would be stronger evidence.
